# Aechronis `combat` — Deep Technical Reference & Bug Audit

Companion to [NODES_DEEP_DIVE.md](NODES_DEEP_DIVE.md), same treatment applied to the gun/melee/vehicle library: three independent, exhaustive read-throughs of the actual `Aechronis/combat` source (master branch), each explicitly tasked with hunting for bugs, races, and exploits rather than just describing behavior. No external design doc exists for `combat` the way nodes.soy did for `nodes`, so this file skips straight to mechanics + bug catalog.

Every finding is cited to a real file/class/function. Severity reflects likely production impact at 200+ concurrent players, not theoretical severity. Two findings below (the explosion thread-safety bug, and the general HashMap thread-safety pattern) were independently discovered by *separate* research passes covering different subsystems — that convergence is itself a signal of how real and pervasive these are, not double-counting.

> **Re-verified 2026-07-30 against current `master` (HEAD `5a628df`, "feat: change camara distence based on vehicles hitbox for all but drones").** Nearly every CRITICAL/HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW item below is confirmed **still present, unchanged**, despite 43 changed files since this doc was written (new `Boat.kt`, `Health.kt`, `utils/DamageAttribution.kt`, `utils/VehicleCameraDistance.kt`, `listeners/VehicleListener.kt`, and a disconnect-cleanup refactor). Notable nuances from re-verification:
> - **M5 (no drone disconnect-cleanup) is NOT fixed** by the "move vehicle disconnect cleanup to the single existing listener" refactor — `PlayerDisconnectListener.kt` still only special-cases `Plane`; a disconnecting drone pilot still leaves an ownerless, undespawning drone body. Don't assume a refactor with a promising commit message actually closed a flagged bug without checking.
> - **H7 (unbounded projectile lifetime) is partially mitigated**: `Projectile.kt::onTick()` now despawns on `!instance.isChunkLoaded(nextPos)`, but there's still no absolute lifetime/distance cap — a projectile that stays within a loaded chunk (fired straight up, or looping near spawn) still ticks forever.
> - **M7 (coordinate-flooring `.toInt()` bug) is fixed for the new `Boat.kt` specifically** (uses `floor()` throughout its own surface lookups) but **still present** for `Car`/`Tank`/`Plane` and the shared `Hitbox.checkGroundCollision` path they all use.
> - **New behavior change worth flagging, not just a repeat**: `MannequinDamageListener.kt` now *always* cancels the drone-mannequin's own damage event when forwarding to the pilot, regardless of whether the forwarded hit actually lands (pilot iframe, etc.) — this may have flipped the old LOW-severity "always produces attacker feedback even when rejected" item into the opposite problem (melee attacks on a drone decoy may now produce **zero** attacker-side feedback even on a fully successful forwarded hit). Worth confirming against Minestom's actual `damage()` cancellation-return semantics before treating either direction as settled.
> - See [`research-todo/01-concurrency-model.md`](research-todo/01-concurrency-model.md) for confirmation (via Minestom's own threading docs) that the H1 thread-safety pattern and the C1 off-tick-thread `Explosion.kt` mutation are real, confirmed violations of Minestom's threading model — not just stylistic concerns.

## Part 1 — How it actually works

### 1.1 Guns (`objects/Gun.kt`, `objects/Ammo.kt`, `utils/Ray.kt`)

Guns are configurable data-class items (already known from RESEARCH.md §2): hitscan raycasting via `Ray.kt`'s analytic segment-AABB intersection (a correct, efficient slab-method test — verified sound), ammo tracked through `DataComponents.DAMAGE` rather than NBT specifically to dodge item-swap animations. `Gun.fire()` enforces a server-side cooldown against `Combat.playerLastActionTimes`, checks ammo, and picks the single closest hit among block/entity/vehicle candidates (structurally guaranteed one-hit-per-shot — verified, not exploitable for multi-hit). `Ray.firstEntity` correctly uses each entity's real per-pose bounding box (not a generic fixed size) with no lag-compensation/rewind for fast or laggy targets. Reload is a repeating 100ms scheduled task showing a title progress bar, guarded against concurrent starts, cancelling on item-switch or running dry.

The `sniper`/`automatic` boolean flags exist on `Gun` but — confirmed by tracing every reference in the repo — **are never read inside `Gun.fire()` at all**. Their only consumers are cosmetic/client-facing: `AimingListener` skips ADS logic for automatics, and `ModelManager` swaps a scope-overlay helmet and zeroes movement speed while a sniper gun is aimed. Neither flag independently gates fire rate, spread, or damage — see the bug catalog for what this means practically.

Physical (non-hitscan) projectiles — grenades, tank shells, rockets — are a separate system (`objects/Projectile.kt`, `tasks/ProjectileTickManager.kt`): gravity-simulated `ITEM_DISPLAY` entities re-raycasting a short per-tick segment each frame, exploding via `objects/Explosion.kt` on impact.

### 1.2 Melee (`objects/Melee.kt`, `listeners/MeleeListener.kt`)

A from-scratch reimplementation of modern vanilla Java Edition combat, confirmed vanilla-accurate in its core formula: attack-cooldown progress via the real `0.2 + progress² × 0.8` curve (matching vanilla 1.9+'s exact math including the 0.5-tick partial-tick offset), critical hits at >90% cooldown progress while falling/airborne/not-sprinting (×1.5 damage), sprint attacks (extra knockback, cancels sprint), sweep attacks (radius 4, requires `sweepable` + stationary + grounded, flat 1-damage splash matching vanilla). Per-player cooldown tracking is correctly reset on hotbar-slot switching (`CooldownResetListener`) specifically to close the "pre-charge cooldown on a fast item, swap to a slow one for an instant full-power hit" loophole — a deliberate, correctly-implemented mitigation for *that specific* vector (see bug catalog for the vector it *doesn't* cover).

### 1.3 Health, armor, damage-immunity (`objects/Health.kt`, `ArmorPiece.kt`, `Hitbox.kt`, the `Combat` singleton)

Player health is native Minestom `LivingEntity.health`, mutated only through `Combat.applyDamage` — no parallel/desyncable player-health tracker exists. `Health` (a separate class) is exclusively the HP model for *vehicles*, not players. Armor reduction is computed twice, structurally: once via a vanilla-shaped `Attribute.ARMOR` value set on the item (client-facing display), and again via a hand-rolled flat-percentage multiplier in `ArmorProtectionListener` — correctly floor/ceiling-clamped so it can't invert into negative/healing damage, but see the bug catalog for the double-application risk this creates. A shared `Combat.canDamage`/`applyDamage` singleton enforces a 500ms per-entity damage-immunity window across all damage sources (melee, guns, explosions).

`PlayerDisconnectListener` is genuinely thorough — every combat-relevant per-player map/task is confirmed explicitly cleaned up on disconnect (cooldowns, invincibility timestamps, in-progress reload/aim tasks, vehicle exit). This correctness is entirely manual discipline with no structural enforcement, though — a real regression risk for any future per-player state added without remembering to wire it in here too.

### 1.4 Vehicles and drones (`objects/Vehicle.kt`, `Car.kt`, `Boat.kt`, `Plane.kt`, `Tank.kt`, `Drone.kt`)

Not Minestom's native vehicle/passenger system — every vehicle is a plain `ITEM_DISPLAY` entity moved by hand-rolled per-tick physics, with a second invisible `ITEM_DISPLAY` "seat" entity carrying the real player as a genuine Minestom passenger. This mirrors the same packet/fake-entity approach `nodes`' minimap uses. Driver/passenger state lives across roughly a dozen parallel, unsynchronized `HashMap`/`HashSet` companion-object statics.

`Drone` is the most elaborate: a genuine first-person remote-piloting system, not autonomous or a stationary turret. The pilot's real body is hidden and zero-hitboxed, replaced by a mannequin decoy wearing their skin/armor at their original location; the pilot spectates an invisible camera entity riding the drone. `MannequinDamageListener` correctly forwards damage taken by the decoy back onto the real pilot's HP and blocks direct damage to the now-invisible real player — this is deliberately *not* a free-invulnerability exploit, the design intentionally routes vulnerability through the decoy.

## Part 2 — Confirmed bugs & design flaws, by severity

### CRITICAL

**C1. Explosion terrain destruction mutates world chunks from an unsynchronized background thread pool — independently confirmed by two separate research passes.** `objects/Explosion.kt`'s `init` block wraps its entire block-clearing/fire-placement pass in `CompletableFuture.runAsync { ... instance.setBlock(...) ... }`, defaulting to the shared `ForkJoinPool.commonPool()` — running entirely off Minestom's own tick thread. Every tank shell, plane crash, and drone bomb (`Tank.kt`, `Plane.kt`, `Drone.kt` all route through this) spins up its own independent async task calling `instance.setBlock()` concurrently with the main tick thread's simulation, with vehicle physics code reading blocks every tick (`Car.findSurfaceY`, `Hitbox.checkGroundCollision`), and with any *other* simultaneous explosion's own async task hitting overlapping chunks. Minestom's chunk data structures are not designed for concurrent unsynchronized mutation from arbitrary threads. This is a deterministic, reproducible risk under completely ordinary heavy combat (two tanks firing near each other at the same time) — chunk-section races, lost block updates, or crashes. The same async block also broadcasts on the order of 1000+ particle packets per explosion from that background thread, compounding the concern.

**C2. Reload-task tracking is keyed by item-*type* name tag, not stack identity — a confirmed, reproducible ammo-economy exploit requiring nothing but two guns of the same type.** The reload tick callback's cancel-check verifies only "is the currently-held item still *a* gun of this registered type," never "is it *the exact stack* that started this reload." A player carrying two copies of the same gun can: start reloading empty copy A, switch to copy B (same type, already has ammo) before the timer completes, and have the completing reload top off whatever is *currently held* (copy B) to full — while copy A, which actually consumed the single reserve-ammo unit, sits untouched at zero, itself still reloadable later for a second free top-up. This requires no timing luck or thread race, just owning two of the same weapon and tabbing hotbar slots.

**C3. Hitscan raycasts silently pass through unloaded chunks and any block with an unrecognized collision shape — two independent, concrete shoot-through-walls vectors.** `Ray.kt`'s `firstBlockAt` returns "no hit, keep going" (rather than stopping the ray or treating it as solid) for any position in a chunk that isn't currently loaded — and separately, for any block whose registered collision shape isn't castable to the specific `ShapeImpl` type expected, treating it as fully non-colliding rather than failing safe. The unloaded-chunk case is the more concerning of the two: since max ray distance can span a player's entire view-distance radius, and chunk loading genuinely lags under load (exactly the "200+ concurrent players, active combat causing chunk churn" scenario this project is built for), this bug gets *more* likely to manifest precisely when the server is busiest, not less.

**C4. Zero reach/distance validation on the primary melee attack target.** Confirmed by reading `MeleeListener.onEntityAttack` end-to-end — the only distance check anywhere in the file is the sweep system's *secondary*-target filter (which other nearby entities also get splashed), not a check of the attacker's own distance to their actual target. A client that fires an attack packet against any `LivingEntity` at any range receives full server-computed melee damage with no server-side reach enforcement from this library at all. Whether Minestom's own core packet handling imposes any cap independently of this code is unverified and worth checking against the exact pinned version, but this library adds no defense-in-depth of its own regardless.

**C5. Explosion damage (rockets, grenades, tank shells) has zero line-of-sight/occlusion check — this is standard behavior for every explosive weapon, not an edge case.** `Explosion.applyDamage()` computes falloff purely by Euclidean distance to each player/vehicle/mannequin's bounding box in the instance — there is no raycast or occlusion test between the blast origin and any target anywhere in the function. A player behind a thick wall, a floor, or in a separate room takes identical damage to someone standing in the open at the same distance. This is both a guaranteed balance exploit (clearing cover-camping defenders through solid structures) and a routine friendly-fire/grief risk. It also scans every connected player in the instance per explosion (not scoped to nearby chunks/arena), an added cost concern at 200-player scale with frequent explosions.

### HIGH

**H1. Virtually every per-player/per-entity combat state map across the entire library — guns, melee, vehicles, projectiles alike — is a plain, non-thread-safe `HashMap`/`HashSet`/`MutableList`, confirmed independently by all three research passes.** This is the same bug class as `nodes`' top finding (H3 in NODES_DEEP_DIVE.md), but here it's arguably higher-stakes: it backs the actual per-hit damage-immunity gate (`Combat.entityLastDamageTime`/`activeDamage`), the fire-rate cooldown check, the reload-in-progress guard, the driver-seat exclusivity check, and the active-projectile list — every one of these is a check-then-act pattern on unsynchronized shared state, under a threading model (Minestom's per-chunk-parallel dispatch) that does not guarantee the single-threaded execution this code implicitly assumes. At 200 concurrent players in active combat, two attacks landing in the same tick window on different chunk-threads is a routine occurrence, not a rare edge case. Recommend converting every one of these to `ConcurrentHashMap`/concurrent collections as a baseline fix, independent of any specific exploit.

**H2. `sniper`/`automatic` are cosmetic flags with no actual fire-rate enforcement — a macro can fire any "non-automatic" or "sniper" weapon at full-auto speed.** Since neither flag is read in `Gun.fire()`, the only real rate limiter for every gun regardless of type is the shared `cooldown` value, which trusts however often the client sends fire-triggering packets. There is no discrete "was this a fresh click vs. held-down spam" signal anywhere. This directly undermines the musket-style weapon balance design (RESEARCH.md §2) — a "musket" is only slow-firing as long as the client cooperates.

**H3. Vehicle-mounted plane guns explicitly bypass both cooldown and ammo entirely.** `Plane.fireGuns()` calls `gun.fire(player, firePos, ignoreCooldown = true, ignoreAmmo = true)` — meaning plane weapon fire rate is gated *only* by how often the tick loop invokes it (up to 20 shots/sec per fire point while a key is held), with infinite ammo, compounding H2's weapon-balance concern specifically for vehicle combat.

**H4. Critical-hit eligibility is derived entirely from client-influenced state with no server-side physics cross-check.** `isPlayerFalling`/`!isOnGround`/`!isSprinting` all trust position/state that Minestom does not independently validate against real server-side physics by default. A modified client can spoof a fabricated fall trajectory or silently toggle sprint state to force `isCritical = true` on every strong hit, for a guaranteed and undetectable +50% damage bonus indefinitely.

**H5. A confirmed, reproducible bypass of the cooldown-reset protection via the off-hand swap key.** `CooldownResetListener` only resets cooldown progress on `PlayerChangeHeldSlotEvent` (hotbar number/scroll switch) — it does not cover `PlayerSwapItemEvent` (the F-key main-hand/off-hand swap). A player can idle with any item until their cooldown for "whatever ends up in the main hand" is already maxed out, then F-swap a heavy weapon in from the off-hand, landing an instant full-power (and critical/sweep-eligible) first hit with a weapon that should have required a windup — this is exactly the loophole the listener exists to prevent, just via a different input than the one it actually covers.

**H6. Vehicle damage silently discards the real computed hit magnitude for every vehicle type except Drone.** `Health.takeHp()` only ever applies a flat, fixed damage value looked up by ammo type — the real, distance-scaled `amount` parameter `Vehicle.takeDamage()` receives (correctly computed elsewhere via blast falloff) is completely discarded for Car/Boat/Plane/Tank. A tank at point-blank range from an explosive takes identical damage to one at the blast radius's edge, and if a designer simply forgets to add a table entry for a given ammo type, that vehicle becomes flatly, silently immune to that entire damage category — no log, no warning.

**H7. Unbounded projectile lifetime — a genuine, slow leak.** Physical projectiles only deactivate on hitting something or leaving a loaded chunk — nothing stops one fired into open sky, or falling through a terrain gap while remaining in a chunk column that stays loaded (e.g. near spawn), from ticking (and re-running a full raycast) forever. Over a busy server's real uptime, this is an unbounded accumulation of "immortal" entities with no cleanup path, compounding H1's thread-safety concern on the same unsynchronized `activeProjectiles` list.

**H8. Cross-instance vehicle-entry bug.** `VehicleTickManager`'s "is this player looking at a vehicle" raycast is never filtered by `Instance` — on any server running multiple worlds/arenas (the planned hub+world-shard split, RESEARCH.md §7-§8), a player can be reported as looking at, and can actually enter, a vehicle that physically exists in a completely different instance, purely from coordinate overlap. This produces broken seating state and likely hard desyncs (the vehicle's seat/camera/mannequin entities all spawn in the *vehicle's* instance, not the player's).

### MEDIUM

- **M1** — Likely double-application of armor damage reduction: a vanilla-shaped `Attribute.ARMOR` value is set on the item *and* a separate hand-rolled percentage multiplier reduces damage again in `ArmorProtectionListener` — needs empirical verification against whether the pinned Minestom build's core damage pipeline already consumes the `ARMOR` attribute internally.
- **M2** — `AimingListener`'s two aim-state writers (right-click ADS timeout vs. a sneak-driven `PlayerInputEvent` handler) fight over the same boolean with no coordination — predicted, reproducible flicker of movement speed and scope overlay for any player who moves at all while aiming a semi-auto/sniper weapon without sneaking.
- **M3** — Vehicle/drone action-bar telemetry has zero rate-limiting or change-detection at all (unlike `nodes`' minimap, which at least hash-gates its packets) — a parked vehicle sends byte-identical action-bar packets 20×/second indefinitely to its occupant; at scale during active combat this is thousands of redundant packets/sec plus matching allocations.
- **M4** — The "fake block" client-rendering trick (`ModelManager`) allocates a brand-new one-shot scheduled `Task` per affected air block, per player, per tick, with zero caching — continuous per-tick task churn for anyone stationary near a vehicle or holding an automatic gun, worse around any base/hangar with parked vehicles.
- **M5** — No disconnect-cleanup exists for drones specifically (only `Plane` is special-cased to self-destruct on disconnect mid-flight) — a pilot disconnecting mid-flight leaves the drone body, camera entity, and payload permanently frozen in the world, ownerless, with no despawn timer and no admin command to clean it up.
- **M6** — `Plane` is completely immune to crash/collision detection for its entire ~10-second takeoff phase — an exploitable window to clip straight through terrain, buildings, or other vehicles with zero consequence.
- **M7** — A coordinate-flooring bug: `Car`/`Plane`'s ground-height and collision lookups use `Double.toInt()` (truncates toward zero) instead of `floor()`, producing off-by-one wrong-block lookups for any negative X/Z coordinate — a very ordinary occurrence on any real map not centered at spawn, and easy to miss in testing if the test map happens to be near (0,0).
- **M8** — `getAmmo()` treats a missing `DAMAGE` data component as near-full ammo — any future code path that strips or repairs that component (anvil repair, mending, a datapack, component normalization) silently grants a full ammo refill with zero trace, since ammo state has no dedicated integrity tag of its own.
- **M9** — `Health.takeHp()` clamps the floor to 0 but never the ceiling to `maxHealth` — currently unreachable given today's fixed config values, but a latent invariant gap that silently over-heals past max the moment any zero/negative ammo-type damage entry is ever configured (e.g. a future "repair" mechanic).
- **M10** — `checkVehicleHit()`'s brute-force 0.25-unit-step sampling re-iterates the *entire global vehicle map* at every step of every shot fired server-wide (not filtered by proximity first) — a real per-shot performance cost distinct from the efficient analytic block/entity raycasts, and the same point-sampling approach can tunnel through any vehicle hitbox part thinner than 0.25 units (thin wings, antennas, glass).
- **M11** — No plausibility/anti-cheat check anywhere on a shooter's reported aim vector — an inherent, unmitigated silent-aim trust gap for the entire hitscan gun system (systemic to the "trust `player.position`" design, not specific to any one function).
- **M12** — Debug hitbox visualization (an admin toggle) broadcasts to the *entire instance*, not just the player who enabled it — a dev accidentally leaving this on in a populated instance would spam every player with continuous particle packets for every vehicle on the field.

### LOW

- Item identity is derived purely from a string data-tag with no integrity binding — safe under normal play, worth remembering only if some other NBT-forging/duplication bug is ever found elsewhere in the stack.
- `ArmorProtectionListener`'s flat percentage reduction applies uniformly to *all* damage types (fall, fire, void), not just combat damage — a deviation from vanilla worth an explicit confirm-or-fix.
- Attacking a drone pilot's decoy clone always produces attacker-side melee feedback (cooldown reset, sound, knockback) even in cases where the forwarded damage to the real pilot ends up rejected by their own iframes — a minor feedback/consistency gap, not a security issue.
- An immunity-bypassing explosive/projectile hit still re-arms the standard 500ms damage-immunity window afterward — meaning a normal follow-up gunshot can be blocked by the iframe the bypassing hit itself just set, an inconsistency in what "bypass" actually means.
- No upper bound on `Particles.particleLine`'s step count — currently only reachable with vehicle-sized dimensions, but no defensive ceiling protects against a future caller or a corrupted hitbox definition turning it into a packet storm.
- The drone pilot's operator-mannequin decoy has no explicit anchoring/no-gravity flag (unlike vehicle seat entities) — if physically displaced by other players, the on-exit teleport back to the mannequin's position has no destination-safety check (could return the pilot into lava, off a ledge, or inside terrain).
- No `maxAmmo > 0` guard on `Gun` — a misconfigured zero value produces `NaN`/`Infinity` silently coerced by `roundToInt()` rather than failing loudly at config time.
- An unreachable defensive null-fallback branch in `isPlayerFalling` (harmless, but a sign the logic wasn't fully reasoned through).
- An exact-tie floating-point edge case in gun hit-priority ordering (block vs. entity vs. vehicle) falls through to "block" rather than the simultaneous entity — essentially a non-issue given real-world ray-distance values never approach the coincidence needed to trigger it.

## Part 3 — Priority order if hardening this before launch

1. **C1 (async unsynchronized world mutation) and C5 (explosion line-of-sight)** — both make every explosive weapon in the game either a stability risk or a guaranteed exploit the moment vehicles/explosives see real use; fix before any playtesting involving tanks, planes, or grenade-style weapons.
2. **C2 (reload ammo-theft exploit) and C3 (raycast wall-passthrough)** — both are concrete, reproducible, no-special-conditions-needed exploits that directly undermine the mining/weapon economy and the fairness of PvP itself; fix before any economy- or PvP-facing playtest.
3. **C4 (no melee reach check)** — closes an existing gap that's cheap to fix (a straightforward distance check) and directly enables reach-hacking today.
4. **H1 (systemic thread-safety)** — needs the same definitive answer NODES_DEEP_DIVE.md already called for regarding Minestom's actual threading guarantees; since it's now confirmed across *two* independent libraries built by the same team, this strongly suggests a single project-wide concurrency pass (converting shared state to concurrent collections) rather than fixing each library in isolation.
5. **H2/H3 (weapon-balance-defeating fire-rate bypasses)** — fix before finalizing any weapon balance numbers (RESEARCH.md's still-open "weapon balance" item), since the numbers you pick don't matter if a macro or a vehicle mount can ignore them entirely.
6. **H4/H5 (spoofable crits, cooldown-reset bypass)** — both routine, low-effort-to-trigger PvP integrity issues; fix before any competitive PvP content goes live.
7. **H6/H7/H8 (vehicle damage model, projectile leak, cross-instance entry)** — fix before vehicles are exposed to real players across more than one instance/shard, since H8 specifically breaks the moment the planned hub/world-shard split (RESEARCH.md §7-§8) is live.
8. Everything in MEDIUM/LOW — real, but lower urgency; a good ongoing-hardening backlog rather than launch blockers, with M1 (armor double-application) worth an early empirical check regardless since it directly affects whatever damage-balance numbers get chosen.
